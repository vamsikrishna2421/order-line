Order Line

Your restaurant's phone, always answered.

An assistant that picks up every call, takes takeout orders in ordinary conversation, and sends them straight to your kitchen. A person is always one step away.

The problem

Calls arrive exactly when the kitchen is busiest, so they go unanswered — and a caller who can't get through usually orders somewhere else. A 2025 Harris Poll survey of 2,065 US diners found 58% say their calls to restaurants go unanswered, and 69% would give up on the restaurant.

How it works

  1. The guest calls your existing number.Nothing changes for them, and you keep your number.
  2. The assistant takes the order.It understands the menu, knows which choices are required, and asks only about what's missing — size, spice level, a side.
  3. It reads the order back.Item by item, before anything is sent, so mistakes are caught on the call rather than at the counter.
  4. The order reaches your kitchen.Printed, on screen, or into the point-of-sale system you already use.

What's different

Answers all of them

Five calls at once during the dinner rush are five answered calls, not one.

Replies without the pause

Built to answer at conversational speed, so callers don't talk over dead air.

Never invents a price

Totals come from your own system. The assistant doesn't guess what anything costs.

Hands over to staff

Ask for a person and it transfers. Anything it isn't sure about goes to your team, not into the ticket.

You stay in control

Mark an item unavailable, pause the assistant, or read a transcript of any call.

Set up from your menu

Onboarding reads your existing menu rather than asking you to rebuild it by hand.

Honestly

It takes takeout orders in English, and it says up front that it's automated. It isn't a replacement for your staff, and it won't pretend to be a person. Accuracy and response-time results will be published from the pilot, measured rather than claimed.
